# Writing Discussion

> Introduction and Discussion sections of a manuscript tell the story, while the Methods and Results sections report the study

[@Lingard2017-qb; @Lingard2016-cs]

## Articles on Writing the Discussion Section

Below are several peer-reviewed articles (including review and commentary pieces) from the past decade that offer frameworks, guidelines, and best practices for writing an effective Discussion section in medical education research manuscripts. Each reference is accompanied by full citation details and a summary of its key recommendations:

### 1. Discussing discursive discussions [@Walsh2016-va]

@Walsh2016-va reviews common pitfalls in medical education papers’ Discussion sections and provides clear guidance on what to include. He emphasizes that a good Discussion should

- Good Discussion
    - directly relate to the study’s results
    - offer a **balanced appraisal of the study’s strengths and weaknesses**
    - realistically assess the findings’ significance and applicability
    - consider the **feasibility of implementing the results in practice

- Typical errors
    - **minimizing the study’s limitations or overstating its strengths**
    - introducing new arguments or concepts late in the Discussion
    - conversely, prematurely “closing down” the discussion
    - getting carried away with overly optimistic claims not grounded in the data

### 2. Does your discussion realize its potential [@Lingard2017-qb]

In this open-access “Writer’s Craft” article, @Lingard2017-qb argues that the Discussion should

> “bring to fruition the story that the Introduction started,”

thereby creating a coherent narrative of the research. Too often, she notes, authors default to a formulaic approach—merely

- common structure (that fails to realize the paper's potential)
    - summarizing results
    - acknowledging limitations
    - suggesting future research

which, while common, **fails to provide a compelling narrative arc or fully realize the paper’s potential**. Lingard proposes two strategies to enhance the Discussion’s persuasiveness and coherence: 

1) Treat your key ideas as “characters in a drama
    - ensure the concepts raised in the Introduction reappear and evolve in the Discussion rather than introducing new main ideas
2) Deliberately align the Discussion’s structure with the Introduction’s storyline

She describes three possible narrative structures for Discussions (“coming full circle,” “deep exploration,” and “surprise insight”), each linking back to how the Introduction framed the problem. By using a storytelling approach—returning to the paper’s **main themes (“characters”) and the promise made in the Introduction**—authors can craft Discussion sections that are more engaging, cohesive, and impactful.

### 3. The art of limitations [@Lingard2015-js]

This article, also part of the “Writer’s Craft” series, zeroes in on the **Limitations** part of the Discussion, offering a framework for how to present study limitations effectively. Lingard identifies three common rhetorical approaches authors take with limitations: 

- **Reflection**
    - which she advocates as the optimal approach
    - the author thoughtfully analyzes how specific limitations or design choices create uncertainty in the findings and discusses the implications of those uncertainties
- Confessional
    - in which authors simply list study flaws hoping for forgiveness
- Dismissal
    - in which limitations are mentioned but then downplayed or excused

@Lingard2015-js argues this reflective mode is the only one that truly meets the purpose of a limitations section: it provides a **considered argument about the study’s uncertainties and what they mean for interpreting the results and applying them**. Though adopting a reflective limitations section may require more space and invite scrutiny, it **strengthens the Discussion by honestly contextualizing the findings’ trustworthiness and guiding readers on appropriate application of the results**. In practice, this means not just admitting limitations or brushing them off, but **explaining how limitations affect the conclusions and suggesting how future research or readers might address these gaps**.

### 4. Writing for publication in medical education in high impact journals [@Azer2014-xx]

In this comprehensive guide, Azer and colleagues provide detailed advice on structuring each IMRAD section of a medical education paper. Their recommendations for the Discussion section align with best practices: authors should 

- mirror the structure of the Results section
- addressing each major finding in turn
- **findings must be interpreted to tell a coherent story**
    - explaining what the results _mean_
    - why they matter in the context of existing knowledge

The article stresses distinguishing **statistical significance from practical or educational significance**. Authors are advised to **compare their results with prior literature**, highlighting how their study contributes new knowledge or insights to the medical education field. Importantly, **both strengths and limitations of the study should be openly discussed** in the Discussion, and authors are cautioned against over-generalizing the findings beyond what the data support. Finally, the limitations section can serve as a **springboard for future research**, by acknowledging study limitations and suggesting how subsequent studies might build on the work or address remaining questions. In summary, this guide urges medical education authors to write Discussions that interpret results in context, acknowledge study quality (strengths/weaknesses), assess the **implications for practice and theory**, and propose **future research directions** grounded in the study’s limitations.
