# Continuing professional development (CPD)

## What?

- Healthcare professionals partake in activities aimed at preserving and expanding their knowledge, skills, and performance [@Al-Omary2024-eb]
- The emphasis of CME is shifting from just improving knowledge to improving physicians’ performance [@Davis2008-ya]

## Why?

- to provide timely, efficient, and state-
of-the-art patient care in an environment where knowledge, technology, and social requirements are rapidly changing.[@Panda2010-rk]
- to address challenges introduced by ageing, changing patient populations, cultural diversity, chronic diseases, care-seeking  behaviour, and heightened public expectations [@Frenk2010-aj]

## Impact

- educational meetings [@Forsetlund2021-qg]
  - Educational meetings include courses and workshops in various formats
  - Compared with no intervention, educational meetings as the main component of an intervention probably slightly improve professional practice and, to a lesser extent, patient outcomes 
- Audit and feedback [@Ivers2012-fu]
  - an individual’s professional practice is measured and then compared to professional
standards. The results of this comparison are then fed back to the individual.
  - generally leads to small but potentially important improvements in professional practice
- Educational outreach visits [@O-Brien2007-pg]
  - Trained people visit clinicians where they practice and provide them with information to change how they practice. The information given may include feedback about their performance, or may be based on overcoming obstacles to change. 
- burnout, growth, or psychosocial climate
  - only few study about them [@Nexoe2024-gj]

### Factors influencing Impact of CPD

- Interactivity
  - more interactive, use more methods, involve multiple exposures, are longer, and are focused on outcomes that are considered important by physicians lead to more positive outcomes [@Cervero2015-wm]
- self-directed learning is effective to increase knowledge [@Murad2010-fm]
  - in systematic review of 59 studies
- didactic lecture alone does not change physician behavior [@Davis1999-do]
